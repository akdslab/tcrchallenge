Why recognition matters

T cells in health
and disease

T cells help protect us from infection and cancer. They can also contribute to disease when immune responses target the body’s own tissues.

01 / Cancer

Recognizing a tumor

T cells can recognize and kill cancer cells. Finding the peptide–MHC targets they recognize can help researchers develop T-cell therapies.

T cells and cancer →
02 / Autoimmunity

Recognizing self

Immune tolerance normally limits responses against our own tissues. When it fails, self-reactive T cells can contribute to tissue damage.

T cells and tolerance →
03 / Infectious disease

Recognizing infection

T cells recognize peptides from pathogens in an MHC context. Some kill infected cells; others help coordinate the immune response.

T cells and infection →

Which peptide–MHC combinations does a T-cell receptor recognize?

Predicting these interactions is a grand challenge in immunology. We are developing open models, experimental methods and datasets to help researchers connect receptor sequences to their targets and test predictions in the lab.
